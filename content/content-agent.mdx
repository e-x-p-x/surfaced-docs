title: "Content Agent"
description: "The agent that watches for content opportunities, and what it is and is not allowed to do on its own."
---

Content Agent watches your results for work worth doing and prepares it. It is **off by default**, and it never publishes anything on its own.

Find it under **Workspace settings → Content → Content Agent**. The Briefs page also shows its current mode (for example _Content Agent · Ask me first_), with a shortcut to its settings.

## Three states, not two

| State | What runs |
| --- | --- |
| **Off, watching** | The observer only. It records opportunities and counts them, and makes no model calls and no spend. You will see a line like "Content Agent spotted 12 opportunities — turn it on to prepare them". |
| **On** | Planning, approvals, execution and the notification emails. |
| **Off, stopped** | Nothing at all, not even the observer. |

"Off, watching" is the default for a workspace that has never configured it. That is deliberate: you get the count of what it *would* work on before you let it do anything.

Turning it on resumes from the settings you last saved.

## What it may do without asking

Choose the **Execution depth** in the Content Agent settings:

| Mode | What it may do |
| --- | --- |
| **Ask me first** (default) | Finds and prepares work, but you review and approve each action before it starts. This is the only mode that can also prepare improvements to existing pages. |
| **Automatic drafting** | Creates low-risk article drafts on its own, within your limits. Page improvements are off. Nothing goes live automatically. |
| **Send to CMS** (only where available) | Sends verified drafts to a connected Shopify store as hidden drafts. Shown only when the Shopify handoff is available and a store is connected. |

<Note>
  In every mode, nothing is published by Content Agent. Article drafts stay in Surfaced until a person publishes them through the hosted blog, or copies or exports them to their own website or CMS. Changes to existing pages stay in Surfaced until they are separately synced.
</Note>

## Limits and scope

- **Content items per week.** A weekly maximum (for example 25). Content Agent spreads its automatic starts evenly within that cap and within your remaining monthly content allowance. Articles and completed page optimizations share the same allowance.
- **Paid overages.** An option to let Content Agent go beyond your included allowance, where your organization has overages enabled.
- **Content to prepare.** Which kinds of work it may prepare: Create Articles, Prepare page rewrites, Improve metadata, Add FAQ or schema, Improve internal links.
- **Markets.** Which of your workspace markets it may write for.

**Generate now** starts queued work sooner; it still uses your allowance and needs the same approvals.

## Who can turn it on

Owners and managers, and nobody else. Editors can act on individual pieces of work while it is on, but cannot switch it on or off.

Saving the settings form never flips the switch. If it was off, it stays off; if it was on, it stays on. The one exception is the first-time **Activate Content Agent**, which is an owner or manager opting in.

## Why it sometimes goes quiet while switched on

Planning pauses for a workspace where no member has signed in for 21 days. The observer keeps recording, so nothing is lost, but no new work is prepared until someone comes back. You are not shown this, so if a long-idle workspace looks like it has stopped producing work, that is usually the reason.

## Where its work turns up

Prepared work arrives in [Actions](/improve/actions) like anything else, with the source it came from. Approving an item is what releases it to be generated.

## When to switch it on

Leave it watching for a week first. The count it reports is a free read on how much work it would create, and it costs nothing. If that number is one you would actually work through, turn it on in the default mode and approve the first few by hand before loosening anything.
