Articles is everything that has been written, at whatever stage. Each one is made of blocks — paragraphs, headings and the rest — and carries its own history.

## Reviewing a draft

A draft arriving from a [brief](/content/briefs) run is finished text, not an outline. The review question is not "is this written" but "is this true, and is it ours".

Three things to look at, in order:

**The fact-check.** Every article carries a fact-check with a score out of 100 and a list of issues. Issues can be dismissed when they are wrong, and a dismissal is remembered — the report shows what was dismissed rather than quietly dropping it.

**The claims nobody can source.** The checks catch contradictions and unsupported assertions; they cannot catch a statement that is plausible, unsourced and wrong about your own product. That part is yours.

**Whether it sounds like you.** Brand voice is configured in workspace settings, and it gets most of the way there. The last stretch is editing.

## Editing

Block content is edited in the app. Metadata — title, description, excerpt, categories, tags, language — can also be changed through the [API](/api/public-api).

Every saved change creates a revision you can view and restore. Restoring is version-checked, so a restore cannot silently overwrite an edit someone else made while you were looking at an older version.

## Publishing

**Publish** puts the article on your [hosted blog](/publish/hosted-blog) and returns the live URL. It requires the hosted blog to be enabled.

An article that has not passed its checks is not published. That is a hard gate, not a warning: a critical finding makes a piece unpublishable until it is resolved or the finding is dismissed with a reason.

**Unpublish** takes it off the blog and leaves the article in the workspace. Nothing is lost; the URL stops resolving.

## Archiving

Deleting an article archives it: it leaves the default list and keeps its history. Archiving something already archived just refreshes the timestamp rather than failing.

Prefer archiving to deleting. An article that stopped being true is evidence of what you used to say, and it costs nothing to keep.

## Did it work?

Publishing is not the end of the loop. Whether a page is being cited by the assistants is on [Content performance](/insights/content-performance), and it takes time — the engines have to re-read your site, and then a tracking run has to ask the questions again.
