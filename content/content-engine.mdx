Everything that decides _how_ a brief turns into an article lives in **Workspace settings** (sidebar footer), under **Content** (Brand voice, Templates) and **Targeting** (Target markets, Audiences & categories, Competitor rules), rather than on the brief itself. A brief says what to write; these say how it comes out.

## Voice and format

**Brand voice** defines how your content sounds and feels. It is what gets a draft most of the way to sounding like you. The remainder is editing, which [Articles](/content/articles) covers.

**Article templates** are reusable configurations per article type: the shape a given kind of piece takes, so that a comparison page and a how-to are not generated to the same recipe.

## Targeting

**Target markets** is where you sell. It seeds the defaults for new prompts and shapes generated content, and it is a different setting from the per-prompt country on [Prompts](/insights/prompts). [Markets and languages](/set-up/markets-and-languages) explains why the two exist and which one produces your numbers.

**Audiences & categories** holds your target-audience presets and content categories, which a brief falls back to when it does not name its own.

**Competitor rules** governs how rivals may be referred to in generated content.

## Knowledge

The material the generator draws on has its own page: [Knowledge](/content/knowledge).

## Advanced

**Search playground** lets you query your own knowledge base directly, which is the quickest way to find out whether a weak draft was caused by missing source material.

**Release history** lists the engine's own releases.

## Which of these to set first

Brand voice, then target markets. Those two change every draft you will ever generate. Templates matter once you are producing more than one kind of article, and the rest can wait until something you read in a draft makes you want them.

A brief that falls back to good workspace settings is usually better than a brief that overrides them, because the settings have been corrected over time and the brief was written in one sitting.
