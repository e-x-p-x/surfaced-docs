---
title: "Members and roles"
description: "Who can do what, in the organization and in each workspace, and how the two sets of roles fit together."
---

Surfaced has two levels, and each has its own roles.

An **organization** is the billing entity. It owns workspaces and holds the plan.

A **workspace** is one brand being tracked, with its own prompts, competitors, settings and results. A person can belong to several, with a different role in each.

Both vocabularies use the word "Owner" for different things, so it is worth being precise about which one you mean when you talk about access.

## Organization roles

Set under Settings → Organization → Members.

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Everything: billing, members, every workspace. Can promote or remove other owners. |
| **Admin** | Manage members and workspaces across the organization. **No billing access**, and cannot manage other admins or owners. |
| **Member** | Belong to the organization. Actual access comes from the workspace role they hold in each workspace. |

Billing lives here and only here. No workspace role grants it.

## Workspace roles

Set per workspace.

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Full control: settings, members, content and tracking. Can delete the workspace and manage other owners. |
| **Manager** | Settings, members, content and tracking. Cannot delete the workspace or manage other managers and owners. |
| **Editor** | Create and edit content, tracking, and content-engine settings such as brand voice and templates. Can invite reviewers and viewers. |
| **Reviewer** | Review and grade content; view tracking results. |
| **Viewer** | Read-only access to content and tracking. |

In practice:

- **Changing settings** — owner and manager.
- **Adding or removing prompts and competitors** — owner, manager and editor.
- **Grading content** — everyone except viewer.
- **Inviting people** — owners and managers can invite anyone; editors can invite reviewers and viewers only.
- **Deleting the workspace** — owner alone.

## How an invitation resolves

When someone joins a workspace, their workspace role is seeded from their organization role: an org owner becomes a workspace owner, an admin a manager, and a member an editor.

It is only a default. An admin can set a different workspace role per workspace, which is how you give someone full rights in one brand and read-only access in another.

## Choosing a role

Most teams need fewer owners than they think. Owner matters for deleting a workspace and managing other owners; everything a marketer does day to day — prompts, competitors, settings, content — is covered by manager.

Reviewer is the useful one people forget: it grades content and reads results without being able to change what is tracked. It is the right role for a subject-matter expert or an agency reviewer.

Viewer is read-only, which makes it right for stakeholders who want the dashboard and should not be able to move a number by editing the prompt set.
