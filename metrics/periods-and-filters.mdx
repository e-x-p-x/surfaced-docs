---
title: "Periods and filters"
description: "The date window, the plan history floor and the branded/unbranded control — the three things that change every number on every Insights screen."
---

Every Insights screen shares one filter bar, and three of its behaviours surprise people often enough to be worth knowing before you compare two figures.

## The window is anchored on your last day of data

"Past 30 days" does not mean the 30 days ending today. It means the 30 days ending on the most recent day your workspace has data for.

This is deliberate. A workspace whose tracking paused three weeks ago would otherwise show a month of zeroes and look like a collapse in visibility rather than a gap in collection. Anchoring on real data keeps the chart about your brand instead of about the scheduler.

The page tells you when the window is not current — quietly within a couple of days, prominently after that. If a figure looks stale, read that notice first.

## Trailing presets include one extra day

"Past 7 days" covers 8 calendar days, and "Past 30 days" covers 31. Both ends of the range are inclusive, so today (or your anchor day) and the day 7 before it are both in.

It matters when you reconcile against another tool. A 1-in-31 difference in the denominator is about 3%, which is enough to make two correct systems look like they disagree.

## Your plan floors how far back you can look

Each plan keeps a fixed amount of history. Pick a start date earlier than your plan allows and the range is clamped to the floor — including "everything", which becomes "as far back as your plan goes".

It also decides what you open on: the default period is the longest of 7, 30 and 90 days that your plan can actually serve, so a short-history plan opens on "Past 7 days" rather than on an empty month.

A notice above the table says when you are looking at a clamped window, on Analytics, Sources and Competitors. **[Prompts](/insights/prompts) shows no such notice**, so on a short-history plan that table is floored silently.

One thing is deliberately not floored: the comparison period behind each delta. It is the equal-length window immediately before the current one, computed on the full data, so the change figure stays honest even where the chart cannot reach back that far.

## Branded, unbranded, all

A **branded** prompt contains your brand name: "is Surfaced any good?". An **unbranded** prompt does not: "best AI visibility tracker". They measure different things, and mixing them produces a number that means neither.

Branded prompts return your brand nearly always, because the question already contains it. Unbranded prompts are the ones that tell you whether you are being discovered. On a workspace with both, the gap is routinely the difference between 77% and under 1%.

So the control defaults to **unbranded** — the harder, more useful number. If you have never touched it, that is what you have been reading.

Two consequences:

- **Unclassified prompts are in neither bucket.** A prompt that has not been classified appears only under "All". Switching between branded and unbranded on a workspace with many unclassified prompts will not account for every prompt you have.
- **Most workspaces never see the control.** It only appears once a workspace has at least one branded prompt. Everywhere else the scope is "All", and a note on the page says so.

## Charts plot at most 30 points

Whatever period you pick, a trend chart shows the last 30 data points. A 90-day view does not draw 90 days of line.

The KPI cards above them do cover the whole period, so the card and the chart beside it are answering slightly different questions.

## Filtering by platform changes only some of the numbers

With an AI-platform filter active, brand visibility and citation rate are recalculated in your browser from the filtered platforms. Share of voice, average position, sentiment and the competitor table are not — they keep their unfiltered values.

So a platform-filtered view mixes scopes. Read the two recalculated figures as filtered, and the rest as workspace-wide.

## Filters that do not move every number

On [Sources](/insights/sources), the source-type filter and the search box narrow the table and the citation counts, but not the citation-share figure in the header — that one is computed over the whole period regardless. Filtering to "News & Media" will change two of the three header numbers and leave the third still.

It is not a bug; citation share answers a question about the platforms, not about the subset you are inspecting. But it does mean the header and the table can look inconsistent, and the explanation is the filter.

## Everything refreshes under you

When a tracking run completes, open Insights pages update themselves and show a toast. A number you were reading can change mid-session. If you are reconciling figures with a colleague, agree on the period *and* take the reading at the same time.
