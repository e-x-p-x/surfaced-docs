The hosted blog is somewhere to publish without waiting on a CMS project. Articles go live on a Surfaced-hosted site, optionally on your own domain.

## Turning it on

Enable it from workspace settings → **Blog**, with a title. Surfaced provisions a default hostname and the blog is live.

Enabling is idempotent — doing it twice returns the site you already have — and it has to be done before any article can be published.

<Note>
  The hosted blog is not available on every workspace or every environment. If the Blog section is missing from settings, it has not been enabled for your workspace.
</Note>

## Your own domain

You can serve the blog from a subdomain of your own — `blog.yourcompany.com` — rather than the default hostname.

That is worth doing for one reason beyond appearances: a citation only counts toward your [citation rate](/metrics/citation-rate) if the domain is yours. Content on a Surfaced hostname is content the assistants credit to a Surfaced hostname.

If you do use a separate domain, add it to **Owned domains** in [workspace settings](/set-up/workspace), or its citations will not be counted as yours.

## Styling

Settings cover the title, the domains and custom CSS. CSS is sanitised on save, and anything removed is listed back to you rather than silently dropped.

Removing the "powered by" badge requires a plan with white-label included; without it the request is refused rather than quietly ignored.

## Publishing and unpublishing

Both are per article, from [Articles](/content/articles). Publishing returns the live URL. Unpublishing removes the post and keeps the article.

An article that has not passed its quality checks cannot be published. See [Articles](/content/articles).

## When not to use it

If you already have a CMS your team publishes into, use it. The hosted blog is for teams who would otherwise not ship — a page live this week on a Surfaced hostname is worth more than a page that waits a quarter for a CMS ticket.

The one thing to avoid is splitting a content programme across both without deciding which is canonical. Two versions of the same article on two domains compete with each other for the citation.
